Should Australian Shepherds Be Shaved? Why the Double Coat Stays and What Works Instead

The short answer is no. Here is why the double coat matters, the rare exceptions, and what to do for heat and shedding instead.

Published: 2026-05-21 · Australian Shepherd Club

Should Australian Shepherds be shaved for summer? No. A healthy Aussie's double coat keeps it cooler and protects the skin from the sun. Shaving takes that away and can leave the coat growing back unevenly.

Quick answer:

  • Don't shave a healthy Aussie. The soft undercoat traps air, which insulates against heat as well as cold.
  • The outer coat is sun protection. According to the AKC, it protects against sunburn and insect bites.
  • Shaving doesn't stop shedding. Brushing does. In shedding season the AKC suggests an undercoat rake every two or three days.
  • Exceptions are rare: tight mats against the skin, or a clip your vet asks for.

Why the Australian Shepherd Double Coat Matters

The Australian Shepherd was bred to work livestock across varied terrain and climates. Their coat is not a single layer of hair but a two-part system. The outer coat consists of stiff, water-resistant guard hairs. Beneath this is a soft, dense undercoat that traps air. This trapped air layer acts like a thermal blanket: it keeps warmth in during cold weather and, critically, insulates against external heat in summer. The coat's structure helps regulate body temperature by trapping a layer of cool air close to the skin.

Shaving removes the guard hairs entirely and thins the undercoat to the point of uselessness. The dog loses the ability to moderate temperature naturally. The skin, now exposed, absorbs direct sunlight. An Australian Shepherd in full coat is often cooler than one shaved to the skin because the coat creates shade at the skin level. The AKC's chief veterinary officer, Dr. Jerry Klein, puts it plainly: "Your dog's coat actually acts as an insulator," and shaving it "to reduce shedding or supposedly to keep the dog cool also eliminates that insulating layer of fur" (AKC on shaving your dog).

The guard hairs also shield the skin. The same AKC article notes that when double-coated dogs shed their undercoat in warm weather, the outer coat stays to protect them from sunburn and insect bites. The AKC breed standard describes the Aussie's hair as "weather resistant and of medium length" and says "the undercoat varies in quantity with variations in climate." The coat already adjusts to the season by itself.

Damage to the coat's regrowth is another concern. Once guard hairs are cut during shaving, they may not grow back the same. The undercoat often regrows faster and thicker, creating a patchy, densely matted coat. The result can be a fluffy coat that mats quickly. Dr. Klein warns that shaving "can result in improper hair growth and the possibility of follicle damage."

Coat color can change as an Aussie matures too, and that is normal. Our guide to how Aussie coats shift from puppy to adult covers it.

Summer Heat Myths and the Double Coat

Many owners believe a shorter coat automatically means a cooler dog. This is false. Dogs do not cool themselves by sweating through skin like humans. The primary cooling mechanisms for a dog are panting and, to a lesser degree, sweating through their paw pads. Fur acts as insulation. Removing it forces the dog's body to work harder to regulate temperature.

Consider a comparison between an intact coat and a shaved coat:

Feature Intact Double Coat Shaved Coat
Sun protection UV-blocking guard hairs None: skin exposed to direct UV
Heat insulation Trapped air layer insulates skin No insulation; heats up quickly
Water resistance Repels rain and moisture Absorbs water, skin stays wet
Regrowth quality Normal seasonal shedding Often patchy, matted, or no regrowth
Risk of sunburn Very low High to extremely high

An Australian Shepherd's coat sheds seasonally. Thick undercoat sheds out in spring, leaving a lighter summer coat. This natural process handles temperature changes. Shaving interferes with that schedule. The coat may respond by growing more undercoat to compensate, creating a hotter dog in summer.

If you are worried about heat, focus on these effective cooling methods: provide fresh water and shaded areas at all times, use a cooling vest or mat on hot days, and keep walks to early morning or late evening. Grooming-wise, the best thing you can do is increase brushing frequency during shedding season to remove loose undercoat. A thorough line combing with an undercoat rake can dramatically improve air circulation through the coat without damaging the structure.

Shedding misconceptions: does shaving mean less hair?

A common owner complaint is "my Australian Shepherd sheds too much, so I want to shave them to reduce fur." Shaving does not reduce shedding; it changes how the fur falls out. Shaved hair falls just as much, but the short pieces stick into furniture fibers and clothing more stubbornly. You still have the same amount of dander and allergens in the home.

Brushing works better. A deshedding tool removes the undercoat before it lands on your floor. The AKC grooming notes for the breed recommend weekly brushing most of the year. In shedding season they suggest an undercoat rake every two or three days, followed by a wire brush.

Another misconception is that shaving eliminates maintenance. Actually, a shaved Australian Shepherd requires more intensive grooming. The regrowing coat is prone to matting, the skin needs sunscreen, and the coat must be brushed more often to prevent skin issues. Shaving creates more work, not less.

If you are still considering shaving because of shedding, try these steps first:

  • Use a high-quality undercoat rake or shedding blade weekly.
  • Bathe with a deshedding shampoo during heavy shed periods.
  • Consider a professional deep deshed grooming service.
  • Keep a consistent vacuuming routine for your home.

Rare exceptions: when clipping is the right call

There are very few good reasons to clip an Australian Shepherd, and none of them is looks or convenience:

  • Tight mats against the skin. If the coat is pelted and brushing would hurt, a groomer may need to clip the matted areas.
  • A vet's request. A vet may clip a small patch for a procedure or treatment. Follow the vet. You can ask whether a clip instead of a full shave is possible.

If your dog seems to struggle in the heat, don't reach for the clippers. Move the dog somewhere cool, offer water and call your vet if it doesn't recover quickly.

How to talk to your groomer

Many owners walk into a grooming salon and ask for "a summer cut," which can mean anything. The groomer may perform a breed-specific clip or, if you insist, they might shave the dog. You need to be explicit about what you want and what you do not want.

Do not say: "Shave him down, it's too hot." Say instead: "Please do not shave or clip the double coat. I need a thorough deshedding and a sanitary trim only."

Request these services instead of a shave:

Request What it means
Full deshed treatment Undercoat rake, high-velocity dryer, deshedding shampoo
Sanitary trim Trim around paws, ears, and genital area only
Light outline trim Scissors used to shape feathering and chest, no clippers touching the body

If the groomer suggests a shave, ask why. If they say "it helps with shedding" or "it will keep the dog cooler," question that advice. Seek a groomer who understands double-coat care. Check the price: a proper deshedding costs more than a shave-down because it requires more labor; paying more gets you a healthier coat.

What to Do Instead of Shaving

You have more effective and safer options to manage your Australian Shepherd's coat and comfort. A prioritized list:

  1. Deshed at home: Use an undercoat rake and a slicker brush. Work section by section, line brushing to reach the skin. Spend 15 minutes three to four times per week during shedding season.
  2. Invest in a high-velocity dryer: A pet dryer blows out dead undercoat without damaging guard hairs. Use it outside after brushing. It is faster than brushing alone.
  3. Provide cooling infrastructure: Cooling mats, elevated cots, fans, and always-available water in multiple locations make a difference.
  4. Adjust exercise schedule: Walk early morning and late evening on hot days. Test the pavement with the back of your hand. If it's too hot to keep your hand there, it's too hot for paws. A lighter harness also helps on warm walks: see our Front Range Flex vs original harness comparison.
  5. Use dog-safe sunscreen: For dogs with thin or lighter-colored areas of coat (ears, belly, nose), apply canine sunscreen before prolonged sun exposure.

If your Aussie still seems to overheat after these steps, talk to your vet rather than the groomer. For planning walks around the heat at each life stage, see how much exercise an Aussie needs by age.

The bottom line

Should Australian Shepherds be shaved? Almost never. Deshed instead of shaving, cool the dog with shade, water and walk times rather than clippers, and leave clipping to matted coats and your vet's instructions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it ever okay to shave an Australian Shepherd in summer?

Almost never. The only exceptions are medical—such as surgery, severe matting, or a veterinarian-prescribed treatment for a skin condition. Even then, ask if a non-shaving alternative exists.

Will shaving stop my Australian Shepherd from shedding?

No. Shaving shortens the hair but does not affect the number of hairs that fall out. The short hairs embed in fabric more stubbornly. Deshedding tools and regular brushing reduce floor fur much more effectively.

Does an Australian Shepherd get hotter with long hair?

No. The double coat insulates against heat. Shaving removes the insulating air layer and exposes skin to direct sunlight, making the dog hotter, not cooler.

How long does it take for an Australian Shepherd's coat to grow back after shaving?

There is no fixed timeline, and the coat may not come back the same. The AKC's chief veterinary officer warns that shaving can cause improper hair growth and possible follicle damage, so regrowth is often uneven and prone to matting.

Can I give my Australian Shepherd a summer cut without shaving?

You can request a "light outline trim" using scissors only on the feathering of the legs, chest, and belly. Avoid any clipper work on the body. A deshedding treatment removes undercoat while preserving the protective guard hairs.

What is the best way to keep my Australian Shepherd cool in summer?

Provide cool, shaded areas and fresh water constantly. Use a cooling mat or vest. Walk only during the coolest parts of the day. Brush out the undercoat thoroughly to allow airflow through the coat. Never rely on shaving for cooling.

How often should I brush my Australian Shepherd to prevent matting?

The AKC recommends weekly brushing for most of the year, and an undercoat rake every two or three days during shedding season, followed by a wire brush.

Can an Australian Shepherd get sunburned on shaved spots?

Yes. The outer coat protects the skin from sunburn, so shaved or clipped skin is exposed. Keep a clipped dog out of strong sun and ask your vet about protecting thin-haired areas.
