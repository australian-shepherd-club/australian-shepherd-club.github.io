Ruffwear Front Range Flex Harness Review 2026: Knit Flex vs Original Front Range for an Australian Shepherd

Same size chart, same clips, different fabric: how to pick the right Front Range for a busy herding dog and hot summer walks.

This Ruffwear Front Range Flex harness review answers one narrow question for Aussie owners: should your Australian Shepherd wear the new engineered-knit Front Range Flex or the original, foam-padded Front Range? Both share the same size chart, the same four adjustment points and the same two leash clips, so the choice comes down to fabric. Our short verdict: the Flex suits a lean, sensitive Aussie on warm-weather walks and swims; the original still makes more sense for a strong puller who runs through brush. This is a research-based review built from Ruffwear's published specs, an independent long-term test of the original and owner fitting guidance, not a lab test.

Do not guess the size from your dog's height or weight; the only reliable number is a soft tape measure around the chest. The breed standard covers a wide frame: Wikipedia's Australian Shepherd overview lists 20–23 in at the shoulder for males, 18–21 in for females and 35–70 lb overall, and a frame range that wide means two Aussies of the same height can need different harness sizes.

What is the Ruffwear Front Range Flex harness?

The Front Range Flex is a knit version of Ruffwear's best-known everyday harness. Instead of padded panels sewn together, the body is made as one engineered knit, a method Ruffwear says is inspired by athletic footwear and designed to "move, flex, and breathe."

Key specifications at a glance

  • Body: engineered knit of 75% polyester and 25% nylon, with very few seams and a light loop knitted in rather than sewn on.
  • Leash points: an anodized aluminum V-ring on the back and a reinforced webbing loop on the chest.
  • Adjustment: four points, plus side-release buckles so you do not have to lift paws through loops.
  • Extras: reflective trim, an ID pocket for tags and a loop for a clip-on light.
  • Sizes: XXXS through L/XL. The XXXS (9–13 in girth) is new; the original stops at XXS.
  • Care: machine wash cold on gentle with mild detergent, air dry. The original is hand wash only.
  • Price: Ruffwear's US store lists the Flex at $69.99 and the original Front Range at $59.99.

Who it is for

Ruffwear's own guide to choosing between the Front Range and Front Range Flex points the Flex at sensitive dogs, dogs that spend a lot of time off leash and deep-chested breeds, and points the original at heavy pullers and dogs that need more support. An Australian Shepherd can sit in either camp, which is why the fit finder above asks about pulling and terrain rather than breed alone.

Build quality: engineered knit vs 300D ripstop

The real difference between the two harnesses is the shell. The original Front Range uses a 300D polyester ripstop shell over PE foam padding, lined with a polyester knit mesh. The Flex drops the foam sandwich for a single knit layer.

Original Front Range: firm, padded, abrasion resistant

Ruffwear says the 300D ripstop makes the original "a little more rugged and abrasion resistant," and that it has a firmer feel that molds to the dog after a short break-in period. That matches Active Gear Review's long-term Front Range test, where the reviewer found the harness softened after a few weeks of use without losing function and did not pill.

Front Range Flex: soft from day one

Ruffwear says the knit conforms to the dog's body on the first day, needs no break-in and dries faster than the original after a swim. Fewer seams also means less material stacked up where straps meet. The trade-off is that the company itself rates the original as the tougher fabric, so a knit harness dragged through thorny scrub every weekend will show wear sooner.

Ruffwear's advice is to measure around the widest part of the rib cage and to choose the larger size when your dog sits between two. If you share your home with a smaller Aussie, our mini vs standard Australian Shepherd size guide shows how much overlap there is between the two types.

Measure through the coat, not on top of it

An Aussie's double coat can add a surprising amount to a casual tape reading. Part the fur and keep the tape snug against the body, then measure again in winter coat and after a heavy shed. If the two readings fall on either side of a size boundary, you will appreciate the four adjustment points.

Puppies and adolescents

A harness bought at 5 months rarely fits at 15 months. Aussies keep filling out through the stage our Australian Shepherd teenage phase timeline describes, so for a growing dog pick a size where today's girth sits in the lower half of the band, recheck the fit every few weeks and budget for one more harness later.

Warm-weather walks: which harness feels cooler?

For summer walks the Flex has the clearer design advantage on paper: one thin knit layer instead of ripstop over foam and mesh, built to breathe, and faster drying after a dip. Neither Ruffwear nor the reviews we found publish temperature or airflow measurements, so treat "cooler" as a reasonable expectation from the materials rather than a tested number.

The coat matters more than the harness

A harness covers only part of the chest and back; most of what keeps an Aussie comfortable in heat is the coat, the time of day and the pace. Clipping the coat short to "help" with summer usually backfires, as our guide on why shaving an Australian Shepherd backfires explains. Plan the hard sessions for cooler hours, slow the pace on hot days, and use the shade breaks to check under the straps for damp, matted fur.

Water dogs

If your Aussie swims most weekends, the Flex's faster drying is a practical win: a harness that stays soaked holds wet fur against the skin under the straps for longer. Ruffwear says both versions dry quickly, with the Flex drying faster.

Pros and cons of the Front Range Flex for an Aussie

Pros

  • Soft and flexible from the first walk, no break-in
  • Thin, breathable knit for summer
  • Dries faster than the original after swims
  • Machine washable
  • Same front clip for redirecting pulling
  • Side-release buckles, easy for gear-shy dogs

Cons

  • Less abrasion resistant than 300D ripstop
  • No foam padding for heavy pullers
  • $10 more than the original at US list price
  • No published heat or airflow data
  • Not a lifting or car-restraint harness

The last point applies to both versions. Active Gear Review notes the Front Range is not designed for lifting, skijoring or car security and does not support the mid and hind quarters, so if you need a handle for scrambles, look at a harness made for that job.

Which Front Range should your Aussie wear?

Choose the Front Range Flex if

  • Your Aussie walks mostly in warm weather or swims often.
  • Your dog is touchy about gear, or a padded harness has rubbed behind the elbows.
  • You want a harness you can throw in the washing machine after muddy walks.
  • Your dog is a small mini or a young puppy that needs the XXXS size.

Choose the original Front Range if

  • Your Aussie pulls hard for most of the walk and you want more structure.
  • Your walks involve brush, rock or regular off-trail scrambling.
  • You would rather spend $10 less and accept a short break-in.

Buying tips

Measure first, then buy from a seller with free exchanges: Aussies straddle size boundaries often enough that a swap is common. Check the size chart on the exact product page before you order, keep tags on until you have adjusted the harness at home, and do a short trial walk before you remove them. A harness is a tool, not a training plan; if pulling is the real problem, pair it with the routines in our guide to Australian Shepherd exercise by age, since an under-exercised Aussie pulls harder.

What testers and owners report

We found no independent long-term test of the Flex yet, so here is what the evidence actually covers.

  • Sizing holds up in real use. In Active Gear Review's test of the original, two dogs with 28 in and 30 in girths both fit the Medium, which matches the chart's 27–32 in band.
  • Fit stays put once set. The same reviewer found that once adjusted, the straps stayed put without frequent readjustment; they suggest loosening the neck straps before the first fitting and leaving a few fingers of room under the neck strap.
  • Front clip for distractions. The reviewer used the back V-ring on normal walks and switched to the chest loop around strong scents and unfamiliar dogs, a pattern that suits an Aussie who fixates on movement.
  • Layers need room. Adding a dog jacket required letting the straps out, worth remembering for a winter coat.
  • Knit claims come from Ruffwear. The breathability, no break-in and faster drying claims for the Flex are the maker's own; we have not seen them measured by a third party.

Frequently asked questions

What size Ruffwear Front Range does an Australian Shepherd need?

Measure the chest at the widest part of the rib cage. A 22–27 in girth is a Small and 27–32 in is a Medium; the Active Gear Review testers' dogs at 28 and 30 in both wore a Medium. Between sizes, Ruffwear recommends the larger size.

Is the Front Range Flex the same size as the original Front Range?

Yes. Both use the same girth bands from XXS to L/XL. The Flex adds an XXXS size for 9–13 in girths.

Is the Front Range Flex cooler for summer walks?

It is the more breathable design: a single knit layer instead of ripstop over foam and mesh, and Ruffwear says it dries faster. No published temperature tests exist, so walk at cooler times of day either way.

Is the Front Range Flex good for a dog that pulls?

It has the same chest loop that redirects pulling, but Ruffwear recommends the padded original for heavy pullers and dogs that need extra support.

Can you machine wash the Front Range Flex?

Yes. Ruffwear's care instructions say to secure the fasteners, wash cold on a gentle cycle with mild detergent and air dry. The original Front Range is hand wash only.

How much does the Front Range Flex cost compared with the original?

At the time of writing Ruffwear's US store lists the Flex at $69.99 and the original Front Range at $59.99.

Can my Aussie wear the Front Range for hiking?

For walking and hiking on trails, yes. Neither version is a lifting harness, and the Active Gear Review test notes the original does not support the hind quarters, so it is not the right tool for scrambles where you need to help your dog up.

Final verdict: Front Range Flex vs Front Range for Aussies

Front Range Flex: 8.5/10 for warm-weather Aussies

Same proven fit and leash layout as the original, in a softer, faster-drying knit. Lose a point for lower abrasion resistance and the higher price, and half a point because the comfort claims are not independently tested yet.

For most Australian Shepherds who walk in neighborhoods and parks, swim in summer and are not hard pullers, the Ruffwear Front Range Flex is the better pick of the two. For a powerful puller or a dog who spends weekends crashing through brush, the original Front Range is still the safer buy, and it costs less. Either way, the size decision matters more than the version: measure through the coat, choose the larger size when in doubt and recheck the fit as a young Aussie fills out.
