Australian Shepherd Herding Instinct in an Apartment: Give the Drive a Job Indoors

What herding looks like indoors, which games use the drive, and how to stop heel nipping without fighting the instinct.

Published: 2026-06-01 · Australian Shepherd Club

An Australian Shepherd's herding instinct does not switch off because the "flock" is now two adults, a cat and a hallway. In an apartment it usually shows up as circling, blocking doorways, staring at anything that moves and nipping at heels. You cannot remove the drive, but you can give it a job that fits a small space and keep the dog from practising the habits you don't want.

Quick answer:

  • Herding at home is normal for the breed. Many companion Aussies still try to herd children or other pets.
  • Give the drive a job, not just a longer walk. Ball-pushing (Treibball-style), find-it games, and wait-and-go cues all use the same "move and control" urge.
  • Outdoor time still matters. The AKC breed profile describes a dog that needs "a great deal of exercise on a daily basis".
  • Stop the rehearsal. Gate or leash the dog when heel nipping usually starts, then reward calm and an alternative behavior.

What does herding behavior look like in an apartment?

Herding is controlled movement: the dog gathers, drives or holds a group in one place. Indoors the "group" becomes whoever is moving. The table lists the patterns apartment owners describe most often and the first thing to change.

Why does an apartment Aussie herd the household?

The breed was built for it. The Australian Shepherd Club of America describes the Aussie as "primarily a working dog of strong herding and guardian instincts." Most Aussies now live as pets, but Wikipedia's breed summary notes that many keep a strong herding instinct, and that companion Aussies often try to herd children or other pets.

The AKC breed profile recommends giving the Aussie a job, such as herding, "shepherding children", or sports like obedience, agility or dock diving. It is also blunt about what happens without one: one of the most frequent reasons Aussies end up in rescue is that owners didn't channel the breed's energy through training. An apartment doesn't cause herding. It just leaves fewer places for the drive to go.

Which indoor outlets use the herding drive?

A good indoor outlet asks the dog to move something, or to hold still and wait for your cue. Either way it gets the "move and control" job it wants.

  • Ball pushing (Treibball-style). Treibball was invented in Germany in 2005 by Dutch trainer Jan Nijboer, to keep herding dogs busy where there is no livestock. In competition the dog drives eight large exercise balls into a goal while the handler stays near it. At home, one large ball and a hallway or living-room "goal" is enough to start. Reward nose and shoulder pushes, not biting.
  • Find-it and scent trails. Hide food or a toy, then send the dog to search. It uses the same focus as watching stock and is quiet enough for neighbours.
  • Wait, go and come back. Ask for a sit at one end of a hallway, release the dog to a target and call it back. That is the start-stop control herding needs, at walking speed.
  • Mat work. Reward the dog for lying on a mat while people move around. It teaches the opposite of circling and blocking.
  • Short trick sessions. Spins, backing up and "go around" a chair give a busy brain several small jobs in a few minutes.

Keep sessions short and stop before the dog gets frantic. If the ball game starts to look like non-stop fetch, read why endless fetch makes an Aussie more wired before adding more throws.

How much outside time does an apartment Aussie still need?

Indoor games add to outdoor exercise. They don't replace it. The AKC calls the Aussie "a high-energy, athletic dog" that "needs a great deal of exercise on a daily basis." It suggests at least an hour or two of running time a day, and long walks or hikes with the owner. A yard isn't required. What counts is that someone takes the dog out.

Puppies and adolescents need a different plan from adults. The exercise guide by age covers puppies, adults and seniors. If your dog is between 6 and 24 months, the teenage phase timeline explains why herding and impulse problems often peak then.

How do you stop heel nipping and herding the kids?

  1. Prevent the rehearsal. Use a baby gate, a closed door or a house line at the times nipping usually happens. Every successful nip makes the next one more likely.
  2. Give a job before the trigger. When people are about to get up or run around, ask for the mat or scatter food first.
  3. Reward the alternative. Pay the dog for lying down, looking at you or bringing a toy while people walk past.
  4. Keep children out of the chase. Running and squealing works like moving stock. Teach children to stop and stand still, and have an adult move the dog away.
  5. Get help if it escalates. If nipping turns into hard bites or guarding, stop the games and book a qualified trainer or behaviour professional.

Where can an apartment Aussie try real herding?

Living in a city doesn't rule out stock work. The AKC herding program runs a non-competitive Instinct Test. Dogs can enter from 6 months of age and need no training beforehand. The judge only checks whether the dog can move and control livestock by fetching or driving. The ASCA Stockdog Program has run sanctioned trials since 1974 (the first was in Santa Rosa, California). If a trainer with stock is within reach, an occasional lesson gives the drive its original job.

Common mistakes with herding in a small space

  • Only adding more fetch. Repeating one fast chase builds stamina and craving rather than calm.
  • Punishing the instinct. Scolding a nip doesn't remove the drive. Redirect it to a job you choose.
  • Leaving the window open as entertainment. Hours of watching traffic are hours of practice at tracking movement the dog can never control.
  • No off switch. Every game needs a clear start and end cue, followed by rest.

Frequently asked questions

Can an Australian Shepherd live happily in an apartment?

Yes, if the owner covers daily outdoor exercise and gives the herding drive regular jobs indoors. The AKC describes the breed as needing a great deal of daily exercise, so the plan matters more than the floor area.

Why does my Aussie nip my heels when I walk?

Nipping at heels is a herding move: the dog is trying to speed up or turn something that moves. Prevent it at the usual times with a gate or house line, and reward an alternative such as lying on a mat.

What is Treibball?

Treibball, also called urban herding, is a dog sport invented in Germany in 2005. The dog drives large exercise balls into a goal on the handler's cues. It was designed for herding dogs that have no access to livestock.

At what age can an Aussie take a herding instinct test?

In the AKC herding program, dogs may enter Instinct Tests from 6 months of age, and no prior training is needed.
